A well-fitting suit should not feel like a costume. It should not be so tight that you are afraid to sit down, nor so loose that it looks as though you borrowed it from somebody else.

The best fit is usually the one that looks balanced, feels comfortable and allows you to move without constantly adjusting yourself. That sounds simple, but there are a number of small details that make a very big difference.

## Start with the shoulders

The shoulders are one of the most important areas of a jacket. The shoulder seam should sit where your natural shoulder ends, creating a clean line without collapsing or extending too far beyond the body.

If the shoulder is too wide, the jacket can look oversized and the sleeve head may begin to collapse. If it is too narrow, the jacket may pull across the upper back and feel restrictive.

This is also one of the hardest areas to alter significantly after a jacket has been made, which is why getting the shoulder balance right from the beginning matters so much.

## The collar and chest should sit cleanly

A jacket collar should sit neatly against the back of your shirt collar. There should not be a large gap between the jacket and your neck, but equally it should not feel as though it is pushing into you.

A visible collar gap can be caused by posture, shoulder shape or the balance of the jacket. It is one of those details many people cannot immediately name, but they can usually tell that something looks wrong.

The front of the jacket should also fall smoothly. If the cloth is straining across the chest or waist and creating deep horizontal lines, the jacket is too tight. If there is a large amount of excess fabric, it may be too loose. The aim is to give the body shape without making the garment look forced.

## A fitted suit is not the same as a tight suit

This distinction matters because modern menswear has sometimes confused the two.

A fitted suit follows the shape of the body. A tight suit fights with it.

If the lapels are bowing away from the chest, the button is pulling, the vents are opening or the trousers are creasing heavily across the front, the garment is probably too tight. A suit should create a clean silhouette while still allowing you to breathe, sit, walk and move your arms comfortably.

You are meant to wear the clothes. The clothes are not meant to immobilise you.

## Jacket length matters more than people think

Jacket length has a huge influence on proportion. Too short and the jacket can make the body look unbalanced. Too long and it may overwhelm the wearer.

There is no single correct length for every person because height, leg length, torso length and personal style all play a part. Two people who wear the same chest size may need very different jacket lengths to look balanced.

This is one of the areas where made-to-measure can make a noticeable difference. The aim is not to follow a fixed rule but to create proportion.

## What about sleeve length?

A jacket sleeve should usually allow a small amount of shirt cuff to show. Around half an inch is a common guide, although preference and shirt style both matter.

More important is that the sleeve looks intentional. It should not swallow the hand, and it should not finish so high that it looks obviously short. If one arm is slightly longer than the other, the sleeves should be adjusted individually rather than forcing both sides to match a standard measurement.

## The jacket waist should shape the body

A good jacket should create some definition through the waist, but that does not mean it should be aggressively pinched in.

Some people benefit from stronger waist suppression, while others look better with a straighter line. The right amount of shape depends on the wearer's proportions, posture and personal style.

Good tailoring works with the body rather than imposing a fashionable silhouette regardless of who is wearing it.

## Trousers should sit properly at the waist

Trouser fit is often overlooked because people focus so heavily on the jacket, but badly fitting trousers can undermine an otherwise excellent suit.

The waistband should feel secure without digging in, and the trousers should sit at the intended rise rather than slipping down and creating excess fabric around the crotch. Ideally, they should not need a belt simply to stay up.

A good trouser fit starts at the waist and works down.

## The seat and thigh should look clean

At the back, the trousers should follow the shape of the body without pulling or sagging. Horizontal lines across the seat often indicate that the trousers are too tight, while excess folds can suggest too much cloth or an incorrect balance.

Bodies vary enormously. Some people have a more prominent seat, others are straighter through the back, and some need more room through the thigh. The pattern should reflect the actual person rather than assume everybody is built the same way.

## How much trouser break should you have?

Trouser break is the fold created where the trouser meets the shoe, and there is no single correct answer.

Some people prefer almost no break for a cleaner, more modern line. Others prefer a slight or fuller break for a more traditional look. What matters is that the length works with the trouser width and the style of shoe.

A narrow trouser with too much break can look untidy, while a wider trouser with no break at all can sometimes look too short. Proportion matters more than following a rule.

## Fit should look good when you move

A suit needs to work when you are standing, but also when you are walking, sitting, greeting people and moving through your day.

If a jacket only looks good when you stand perfectly still with your arms by your sides, it is not doing its job. The same applies to trousers. You should be able to sit comfortably without feeling constant pulling across the thigh or knee.

The best tailoring balances appearance with movement.

## Small adjustments can make a big difference

Sometimes a suit does not need dramatic work. A sleeve shortened slightly, a trouser taken in through the waist, a jacket waist refined or a trouser hem adjusted can completely change how the garment looks.

That is why fit is not simply about the size on the label. It is about how the garment sits on the individual.

## The best fit should not distract from you

When a suit fits well, people rarely analyse every detail. They simply think you look good.

The shoulders look clean, the jacket hangs properly, the trousers sit naturally and the proportions feel right. Nothing is fighting for attention.

That is the real goal of fit. Not tightness, not fashion for the sake of fashion, but clothes that work with the person wearing them.

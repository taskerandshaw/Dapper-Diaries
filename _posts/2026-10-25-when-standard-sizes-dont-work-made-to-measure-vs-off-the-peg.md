Buying a suit should not make you feel as though there is something wrong with your body.

Yet for plenty of people, that is exactly what shopping for one can feel like.

The jacket fits across the chest but pulls at the waist. The trousers fit around the middle but are enormous through the leg. The shoulders feel tight, the sleeves are too long, or everything technically fastens but nothing feels particularly comfortable.

That is because off-the-peg clothing has to work around standard sizes.

People rarely do.

## The Problem With Standard Sizing

Off-the-peg suits are designed around predetermined proportions. A particular chest size comes with an expected shoulder width, jacket length, waist and sleeve length. Trousers are built around similarly standard assumptions.

If your proportions happen to sit comfortably within those assumptions, an off-the-peg suit can work very well.

But many bodies simply do not.

You may have broad shoulders and a narrower waist, carry more weight around your middle, have strong thighs, a longer torso, shorter arms or one shoulder sitting slightly differently from the other.

None of those things are unusual.

They simply make buying clothes built to a standard block more difficult.

This is why searches around **MTM versus off-the-peg for people who struggle with standard sizing or are worried about comfort and body shape** come up so often. The question is not always whether made-to-measure is more luxurious.

Sometimes it is simply whether it will make getting dressed easier.

## Made-to-Measure Starts With You

The fundamental difference with made-to-measure is that we begin with the person rather than asking the person to fit the garment.

Measurements are obviously part of that, but measurements alone do not tell the whole story.

We look at posture, shoulder balance, how someone stands and where they naturally need more or less room.

We also talk.

How do you like clothes to feel? What do you normally find uncomfortable? Do you dislike anything feeling close around your waist? Do jackets usually feel restrictive across your shoulders? Do you spend much of your working day sitting down?

Comfort is personal.

A technically well-fitting jacket that makes somebody conscious of it all day is not really doing its job.

For more on the details that make a suit sit properly, see our guide to [how a suit should actually fit](/tailoring/how-should-a-suit-actually-fit/).

## Fit Does Not Mean Tight

One of the most persistent misunderstandings around tailoring is that a well-fitted suit should be a tight suit.

It should not.

A suit should follow your shape without fighting against it.

You should be able to sit down, walk, reach and move normally. Trousers should have enough room through the seat and thigh. A jacket should close cleanly without pulling, but it should also allow you to move without feeling trapped inside it.

This becomes particularly important for people who have previously struggled with standard sizes.

The answer is not to squeeze the body into the smallest possible silhouette.

It is to create balance.

## Your Body Is Not the Problem

Clothes have an unfortunate habit of making people think in terms of what is supposedly wrong with their bodies.

Too short. Too tall. Too broad. Too large. Too small.

Tailoring looks at it differently.

The person standing in front of us is simply the person we are making the clothes for.

Someone may have gained weight, lost weight, have a body shape that changes considerably between their chest and waist, or simply have proportions that mainstream sizing does not accommodate very well.

None of that needs correcting.

The garment needs adapting.

There is something quite liberating about changing the question from, *“What size am I?”* to, *“How should this be made to fit me?”*

## Where Off-the-Peg Still Makes Sense

Made-to-measure is not automatically the answer for everybody.

If you fit standard sizing comfortably, find a suit you genuinely like and only need relatively straightforward alterations, off-the-peg can be an excellent option.

There is no virtue in having something made individually simply for the sake of it.

The difference becomes more significant when standard sizing repeatedly forces compromise.

Perhaps you always have to buy a jacket large enough for your shoulders and then have the waist taken in substantially. Perhaps trousers that fit your waist never fit your legs properly. Maybe you have simply stopped wearing suits because you associate them with feeling uncomfortable.

That is where made-to-measure starts to solve a problem rather than simply offer more choice.

## A Suit Should Let You Forget About It

One of the best signs that a suit fits properly is that after a while, you stop thinking about the fit at all.

You are not pulling the jacket down, adjusting the waistband, worrying about a button straining or wondering whether everybody else can see the part of your body that you are conscious of.

You are simply wearing your clothes.

For a wedding in particular, that matters.

You will be standing, sitting, eating, walking, dancing, hugging people and being photographed from every possible angle. The last thing you should spend the day thinking about is whether your suit feels uncomfortable.

Made-to-measure gives us the opportunity to build the garment around you rather than hoping your body happens to match a standard size.

And that, more than any label inside the jacket, is the real difference.

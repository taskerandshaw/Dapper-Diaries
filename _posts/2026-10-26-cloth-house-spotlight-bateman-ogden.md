One of the nicest parts of choosing a made-to-measure suit is getting beyond simply deciding whether you want navy, grey or something a little more adventurous.

The cloth changes everything.

It affects how the suit hangs, how it feels, how warm it is, how easily it creases and, of course, how it looks. That is why we spend so much time with cloth books in the studio, and why names like Bateman Ogden matter.

Bateman Ogden has been part of the Yorkshire textile story since 1881. Based in Bradford, it has a long history of supplying tailoring cloth and still works closely with British mills today.

What we like about Bateman Ogden is the range.

You can find beautifully traditional suitings, flannels, tweeds and classic business cloths, but there are also more modern performance fabrics, lighter summer options and cloths with a bit more personality.

That matters because there is no single “best” cloth.

The right choice depends on how you live.

If you travel regularly, you may want something with a little more resilience and crease resistance. If you run warm, you may prefer something lighter and more breathable. If the suit is for a wedding, you may want texture or colour that feels special without becoming something you will never wear again.

Bateman Ogden has options across all of those areas.

Their Endurance cloths, for example, are designed with travel and crease resistance in mind, while other collections offer natural stretch, lighter weights and more traditional British structure.

That is the point of having a proper cloth selection in front of you.

You can feel the difference.

A photograph online can tell you whether something is blue or brown, but it cannot really show you how a cloth moves, how soft it feels, how much body it has or whether it has that slightly crisp finish that works so well in a tailored jacket.

Sometimes a client comes in thinking they know exactly what they want and ends up choosing something completely different once the cloth is in their hand.

That is part of the fun.

And it is why we like working with cloth houses such as Bateman Ogden. There is enough choice to stay classic, enough variety to be individual and enough technical difference to make the cloth work for the person wearing it.

The suit starts with the cloth.

Everything else follows from there.

If you have ever looked through a tailoring cloth book, you will almost certainly have seen numbers like Super 100s, Super 120s, Super 150s and thought, quite reasonably, that the bigger number must mean the better cloth.

It does not quite work like that.

The first thing to know is that this is not thread count in the way you might think of thread count in bed linen. It is actually about the fineness of the wool fibre itself. Wool fibres are measured in microns, and as the fibre gets finer, the Super number goes up.

So a Super 150s cloth uses finer wool than a Super 100s cloth.

That finer wool can feel beautifully soft and luxurious in the hand, and it can create a very refined cloth. But this is where people sometimes get caught out, because finer does not automatically mean better for every suit.

## Bigger Is Not Always Better

A Super 150s cloth can be wonderful, but it can also be more delicate. If you are wearing a suit every week, travelling regularly, commuting, sitting at a desk all day or generally asking quite a lot from your clothes, you may actually be much better served by a good Super 100s or Super 120s.

That does not make it a lesser suit.

It simply means the cloth is better suited to the job you need it to do.

This is one of the reasons we never start a cloth conversation by asking, “How high do you want the Super number to be?”

We are much more interested in how you are actually going to wear the suit.

## The Number Is Only Part of the Story

The Super number tells us something useful about the fineness of the wool, but it does not tell us everything about the finished cloth.

It does not tell you how heavy the cloth is, how tightly it has been woven, how much structure it has, how easily it will crease or how it will feel after a long day of wear.

Two cloths can both be Super 120s and feel completely different.

One may feel soft and fluid. Another may feel crisper and more structured. One may be ideal for a wedding suit, while another is far better suited to everyday business wear.

That is why touching the cloth matters.

You can learn a lot from the label, but you learn much more when you actually have the fabric in your hand.

## So What Should You Choose?

For most people, the answer is not to chase the biggest number.

If you want a suit that will work hard and be worn regularly, a Super 100s or Super 120s can be an excellent choice. If you want something softer, finer and a little more luxurious for occasional wear, then moving higher up the scale can make sense.

The important thing is matching the cloth to the person and the purpose.

That is really what all of these numbers are for. They help us understand the cloth, but they should never become a competition.

A Super 150s is not automatically better than a Super 100s.

It is simply different.

And in tailoring, the best cloth is not the one with the highest number. It is the one that works best for you.

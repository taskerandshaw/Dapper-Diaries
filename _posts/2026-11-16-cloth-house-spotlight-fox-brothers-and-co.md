There are some names in tailoring that need very little introduction.

Fox Brothers is one of them.

Based in Wellington, Somerset, Fox has been making cloth since 1772 and has become one of the great names in British textile history.

Mention Fox to somebody who knows cloth and one word will probably come up very quickly.

Flannel.

## The Home of Flannel

Fox Brothers is closely associated with the development of the classic West of England flannel we still know today.

It is soft, beautifully tactile and has a depth that is difficult to reproduce in lighter, flatter cloths. More importantly, it tailors exceptionally well.

A good flannel suit has presence without needing to shout about it.

It drapes beautifully, feels substantial and develops character as it is worn.

That is why proper flannel has remained part of menswear for generations rather than disappearing with whichever trend happened to come along next.

But Fox is much more than grey flannel trousers.

## More Than One Famous Cloth

One of the things we like about working with Fox is the sheer breadth of what is available.

There are classic and worsted flannels, tweeds, jacketing, overcoatings and worsteds, alongside lighter cloths designed for warmer weather and travel.

Fox Air, for example, is a high-twist worsted with a crisp, dry handle. It is breathable, naturally resistant to creasing and particularly useful for somebody who wants tailoring that travels well.

At the other end of the scale are the wonderfully substantial traditional flannels and tweeds that remind you why cloth weight is not something to be frightened of.

Different cloths have different jobs.

The clever bit is choosing the right one for the person wearing it.

## More Than 250 Years of Clothmaking

Fox Brothers grew from a Somerset woollen business into a significant British manufacturer.

At its height in the early twentieth century, the company employed thousands of people and its history includes producing cloth for the British military as well as generations of civilian tailoring.

That heritage matters.

Not because an old date automatically makes a cloth good, but because textile knowledge is built over time.

How a cloth is woven, finished, handled and designed all affect how it eventually behaves when a tailor turns it into a garment.

And that is ultimately what matters to us.

## Cloth Should Have Character

There is a tendency to think that luxury cloth needs to be incredibly fine, incredibly lightweight or accompanied by an impressive number.

It doesn't.

Some of the best cloths are wonderful precisely because you can feel them.

They have texture. Weight. Depth. Personality.

Fox understands that particularly well.

Whether it is a soft flannel suit, a beautifully textured sports coat or a high-twist travel suit, the cloth already has character before we start cutting it.

Our job is to choose the right cloth, put it on the right person and let it do what it was made to do.

Because a great suit starts long before the first fitting.

It starts with the cloth.

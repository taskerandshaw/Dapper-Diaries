Black tie should be one of the easiest dress codes in menswear.

There is a framework. The proportions are established. The details have been refined over generations.

And yet somehow, dinner jackets are also one of the easiest things to get wrong.

The good news is that most mistakes are avoidable.

Here are ten of the biggest.

## 1. Treating a Black Suit as a Dinner Jacket

A black suit with a bow tie is not automatically black tie.

A proper dinner jacket has its own language. Silk-faced lapels, covered buttons, appropriate trousers and eveningwear detailing all separate it from an ordinary business suit.

The difference can be subtle.

But subtle is rather the point.

## 2. Choosing the Wrong Lapel

For traditional black tie, a peak lapel or shawl collar is usually the strongest choice.

Both have the elegance and formality that eveningwear needs.

A notch lapel can work, but it tends to make a dinner jacket feel much closer to an ordinary lounge suit.

If you are commissioning something specifically for black tie, there are better options.

## 3. Assuming It Has to Be Black

Black is classic.

It is not your only option.

Midnight blue has a long and entirely legitimate history in eveningwear and can look particularly beautiful under artificial light.

Ivory also has its place, especially for warm climates, cruises and summer eveningwear.

And velvet is not some modern novelty.

A beautifully cut velvet dinner jacket is a proper tailoring staple and can be one of the most elegant choices in the room.

The important thing is not simply the colour or cloth.

It is whether the whole thing works together.

## 4. Going Too Shiny

A dinner jacket should have contrast.

That is why silk or grosgrain facings work so beautifully against the main body cloth.

But contrast is not the same thing as turning the entire outfit into a reflective surface.

Too much shine very quickly stops looking luxurious and starts looking theatrical.

There is a difference between eveningwear and stagewear.

## 5. Wearing It Too Short

Modern fashion has pushed jackets shorter over the years.

That can work in some styles, but black tie needs balance.

A dinner jacket should have enough length to create a clean, elegant line through the body.

Cut it too short and the proportions start to feel awkward very quickly.

You want elegance.

Not a jacket that looks as though somebody has accidentally put it in the tumble dryer.

## 6. Forgetting About the Trousers

People spend so much time thinking about the jacket that the trousers become an afterthought.

They should not.

Traditional dinner trousers are usually cut cleanly, without belt loops, and often feature a silk braid down the outside seam.

The rise, break and overall line matter enormously.

A beautifully fitted jacket sitting above badly fitted trousers is still a badly dressed man.

## 7. Choosing the Shirt Last

The shirt is not simply something white to put underneath.

It frames the face, affects the line of the jacket and can completely change the character of the outfit.

A Marcella-fronted shirt, a pleated front or a beautifully simple evening shirt can all work.

The important thing is that it belongs with the rest of the outfit.

A standard business shirt can make even an excellent dinner jacket feel slightly unfinished.

## 8. Trying Too Hard With the Accessories

Black tie does not need rescuing.

It does not require six statement pieces, novelty cufflinks, an oversized watch, an elaborate waistcoat and a bow tie visible from the other side of the room.

Choose the details carefully.

A proper bow tie, elegant studs or cufflinks, a restrained pocket square and the right shoes are usually more than enough.

The tailoring should be doing most of the work.

## 9. Ignoring the Shoes

Shoes can undermine the whole outfit surprisingly quickly.

Black tie calls for elegance.

Patent shoes are traditional. Highly polished black leather can work beautifully. Evening slippers have their place too.

What usually does not help is treating the shoes as an opportunity to introduce a completely unrelated colour.

Your dinner jacket does not need tan brogues.

It really doesn’t.

## 10. Forgetting That Fit Matters More Than Almost Everything Else

You can follow every black tie convention perfectly and still look wrong if the fit is poor.

The shoulders should sit cleanly. The jacket should close properly. The sleeve length should be right. The trousers should fall properly.

You should be comfortable enough to move, sit, eat, drink and enjoy yourself without constantly adjusting your clothes.

That is the bit people sometimes forget.

Black tie is formal.

It should never feel like fancy dress.

A good dinner jacket should make you feel sharper, taller and more confident the moment you put it on.

Get the fit right, keep the details considered and resist the urge to improve something that has already had more than a century of refinement.

Sometimes the cleverest thing you can do is leave it alone.
